\begin{abstract}
\noindent This paper revisits the long-term effects of early-life exposure
to Indonesia's Village Midwife Program (\emph{Bidan di Desa}) on adult
human capital, using \NPrimary~individuals across \NVillages~communities
born in 1984--1999 from the Indonesia Family Life Survey waves 1--5.
Treatment is exposure to a village midwife in utero or before age three,
identified from staggered community-level rollout dating from 1989.
The two-way fixed-effects estimator finds small and statistically
imprecise main effects across composite indices for adult health,
cognition, depression, and Big-Five personality. Pre-trend tests reject
parallel-trends in three of four outcome families, and Oster (2019)
sensitivity bounds suggest the main estimates are fragile to selection on
unobservables. Two findings nonetheless survive a layered identification
stack. First, a within-mother specification that exploits sibling
differences in exposure timing produces a depression-index point estimate
of \CoefDepSibFE\ standard deviations (SE \SEDepSibFE), nearly twice the
naïve TWFE estimate; this pattern is consistent with downward bias in
TWFE coming from non-random program placement in poorer or sicker
villages. Second, the gender-specific pattern documented in earlier work
holds: health-index gains concentrate among girls. Together, the results
suggest that the program's effects on later-life human capital are
real but smaller than recent staggered-DiD estimates have implied,
and that within-family designs offer a useful corrective when
program-placement bias is severe.\\

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} early-life intervention; Indonesia;
village midwife; difference-in-differences; sibling fixed effects;
human capital.\\

\noindent \textbf{JEL codes:} I18, I15, J13, O15.
\end{abstract}
