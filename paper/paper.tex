\begin{abstract} \noindent This study examines the long-run effects of Indonesia's Village Midwife Program (\emph{Bidan di Desa}) on adult human capital using a non-migrant sample of children born in 1984--1999 and followed into adulthood in the Indonesia Family Life Survey. Treatment is exposure to a village midwife in utero or before age three, identified from the staggered community-level rollout beginning in 1989. Under the heterogeneity-robust Callaway--Sant'Anna estimator, which uses never-treated communities as the clean comparison group, early-life exposure raises the adult health index by 0.31 standard deviations, an effect that is statistically significant at the five-percent level. The effect is concentrated in the in-utero and age 0--3 windows emphasised by the critical-periods literature and is consistent with the program's stated service bundle. The canonical two-way-fixed-effects estimator produces a near-zero point estimate on the same sample, a gap that is consistent with the staggered-rollout bias of \textcite{goodmanbacon2021} and that illustrates why the heterogeneity-robust estimator is the appropriate headline for this design. A mechanism analysis of mother-reported pregnancy-history data traces a non-trivial share of the program's health effect to a higher probability of skilled birth attendance, the component of the service bundle most directly delivered by the village midwife. Effects on cognition and depressive symptoms are positive but statistically indistinguishable from zero; the socioemotional-trait index is the one domain on which the program's estimated effect is not positive.\\

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} early-life intervention; Indonesia; village midwife; difference-in-differences; sibling fixed effects; human capital.\\

\noindent \textbf{JEL codes:} I18, I15, J13, O15. \end{abstract}
