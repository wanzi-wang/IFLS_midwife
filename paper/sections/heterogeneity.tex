\section{Heterogeneity} \label{sec:heterogeneity}

The pooled estimates of Section~\ref{sec:results} mask substantial heterogeneity across subgroups. Three dimensions are reported.

\paragraph{By sex.} Table~\ref{tab:main_results_by_sex} reports the main specification estimated separately on the male and female subsamples. None of the four index coefficients reaches conventional significance on either subsample; the socioemotional index on girls is the only marginally significant cell at the ten-percent level. The sign pattern on the health index, a small positive point estimate for boys and a small negative point estimate for girls, runs in the opposite direction to the gender-specific pattern documented by \textcite{frankenberg2005} on earlier IFLS waves and a non-migrant subsample, and illustrates that widening the analysis sample to the full preceding-wave variant dissolves the earlier gender-specific health finding. The depression-index point estimates are somewhat larger for girls than for boys but neither reaches significance, and the gap between them is well within sampling variation. Read as a whole, the sex split therefore does not recover a robust gender-specific effect at the precision the present sample permits.

\paragraph{By mother's education.} Table~\ref{tab:het_medu} splits the sample by whether the mother's years of schooling are above or below the median in the analysis sample (six years). The depression-index point estimate is larger among children of less-educated mothers, consistent with the program being a substitute for, rather than a complement to, maternal education in the production of child mental health. The health-index split is flatter and the cognition-index split shows no clear pattern.

\paragraph{By exposure window.} The critical-period evidence is most cleanly read off the CS event study of Figure~\ref{fig:event_study} in Section~\ref{sec:results}, rather than from a stand-alone age-window table. The post-treatment event-time coefficients on the health index sit above zero and are largest in the in-utero and age 0--3 windows, then decay toward zero for older children at the time of the midwife's arrival. A separate TWFE regression with age-window dummies inside community and birth-year fixed effects is not informative here because the dummies are mechanical functions of birth year times village rollout year and become nearly collinear with the cell fixed effects.

The forest plot in Figure~\ref{fig:forest} summarizes the four outcomes across all five subgroups: full sample, male, female, low-education mother, high-education mother.

\paragraph{Why might cognition fail to respond?} The absence of a measurable cognitive effect is consistent with the distinction between fluid and crystallised cognitive abilities discussed by \textcite{tuckerdrob2022}: fluid abilities, measured by the IRT number-series score, begin to decline with age in early adulthood, while crystallised abilities continue to accumulate. The mean age of the analysis sample at wave~5 is~22, by which point any cognitive premium from early-life nutrition or care may have begun to attenuate, and a complementary reading is that the cognitive scales available in IFLS are noisy proxies for the underlying construct.
