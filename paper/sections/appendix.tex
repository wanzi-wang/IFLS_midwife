\section{SAR vs.\ PKK rollout disagreement}
\label{app:rollout}

The Indonesia Family Life Survey records two independent measures of the year a village midwife first arrived in each community: the SAR module, completed by a facility informant, and the PKK module, completed by the village leader.
Both are recorded in waves 2--5.
Table~\ref{tab:rollout_compare} reports diagnostic statistics on the agreement between the two measures across the 153 of \NVillages~analysis-sample communities for which both measures are available.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
\centering
\caption{SAR vs.\ PKK rollout-year agreement.}
\label{tab:rollout_compare}
\input{tables/rollout_sar_vs_pkk.tex}
\end{table}

The disagreement is severe.
The Pearson correlation between the two measures is essentially zero ($\rho = 0.02$), the two sources disagree on the rollout year for roughly 83 percent of the communities for which both are recorded, and the mean absolute disagreement exceeds four years.
The main analysis uses the SAR measure on the rationale that the SAR informant, typically a facility staff member, is closer to the operational record than the village leader, and the alternative PKK-based estimates appear in column~R2 of the robustness table.

The implication of this disagreement for the main estimates is classical attenuation toward zero: were the true rollout year measured perfectly, tighter point estimates and narrower confidence intervals would be expected.
The size of the attenuation cannot be recovered without an independent benchmark.
The disagreement is a feature of the underlying data rather than a coding artifact; both measures pass the same internal consistency checks (valid range, monotonic within-village across waves), and the disagreement is not concentrated in any one province or rollout-year bin.

\section{Pre-trend joint Wald tests}
\label{app:pretrend}

A standard event-study specification with one coefficient per event time and the same community-of-birth, birth-year, and survey-wave fixed effects as equation~\eqref{eq:twfe} produces pre-period coefficients $\delta_{-4}$ and $\delta_{-2}$ for each outcome. Event-time $-3$ is excluded from the pre-trend test because only a thin set of cohort-village cells contributes to that lead (children born in or before $\text{start}_c - 4$ in each treated community), so the resulting coefficient is too imprecise to bear weight as a pre-trend test.
Table~\ref{tab:pretrend} reports the Wald test for joint significance of $\delta_{-4}$ and $\delta_{-2}$ separately for each of the four composite outcome indices, with the null hypothesis that both pre-treatment coefficients equal zero.
The test fails to reject at $\alpha = 0.05$ for all four outcomes, supporting the parallel-trends assumption used by the CS estimator across the four outcome margins.

\section{Geographic distribution of the village-midwife rollout}
\label{app:indonesia_map}

Figure~\ref{fig:midwife_map} shows the geographic distribution of the Village Midwife Program rollout across the IFLS-13 provinces at three snapshot years: 1990 (one year after the program was announced), 1994 (mid-rollout), and 1999 (end of the cohort window used in the analysis).
Each province is shaded by the share of IFLS communities of birth in that province with at least one village midwife by the indicated snapshot year.
The rollout is rapid in Bali and the densely populated Java provinces, with most provinces reaching 30--60 percent coverage by 1994 and 60--90 percent by 1999.
The non-IFLS-13 provinces are drawn in white to indicate that they do not enter the analysis sample.

\section{Multiple-inference correction (Romano--Wolf)}
\label{app:romano_wolf}

The four composite outcome indices are correlated and constitute a four-outcome family.
The headline cell-by-cell standard errors do not adjust for the implied multiple-comparison burden.
Table~\ref{tab:romano_wolf} reports the \textcite{anderson2008}-style Romano--Wolf stepdown $p$-values across the four outcomes, computed from $B = 1{,}999$ pair cluster bootstrap draws at the community-of-birth level.
The specification is the two-way-fixed-effects column of Table~\ref{tab:main_results} with mother controls.
The stepdown enforces monotonicity in the order of observed $|t|$-statistics, so $p_{\mathrm{RW}}$ values are weakly larger than nominal $p$-values for the same coefficient.
