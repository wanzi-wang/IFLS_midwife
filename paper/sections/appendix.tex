\section{SAR vs.\ PKK rollout disagreement}
\label{app:rollout}

The Indonesia Family Life Survey records two independent measures of the year a village midwife first arrived in each community: the SAR module, completed by a facility informant, and the PKK module, completed by the village leader.
Both are recorded in waves 2--5; PKK is additionally recorded in wave~3.
Table~\ref{tab:rollout_compare} reports diagnostic statistics on the agreement between the two measures across the 153 communities for which both are available.

The disagreement is severe.
The Pearson correlation between the two measures is essentially zero ($\rho = 0.02$), the two sources disagree on the rollout year for roughly 83 percent of the communities for which both are recorded, and the mean absolute disagreement exceeds four years.
The headline analysis uses the SAR measure on the rationale that the SAR informant, typically a facility staff member, is closer to the operational record than the village leader, and the alternative PKK-based estimates appear in column~R4 of the robustness table.

The implication of this disagreement for the headline estimates is classical attenuation toward zero: were the true rollout year measured perfectly, tighter point estimates and narrower confidence intervals would be expected.
The size of the attenuation cannot be recovered without an independent benchmark.
The disagreement is a feature of the underlying data rather than a coding artifact; both measures pass the same internal consistency checks (valid range, monotonic within-village across waves), and the disagreement is not concentrated in any one province or rollout-year bin.

\section{Disaggregated outcome components and Anderson $q$-values}
\label{app:anderson}

Each of the four composite outcome indices used in the headline analysis is constructed by Anderson-weighting across its component measures, following \textcite{anderson2008}.
The weights down-weight redundant information across correlated components and deliver a single summary per domain, but the aggregation also obscures the behaviour of the individual components that go into the index.
Table~\ref{tab:anderson_components} reports the component-by-component two-way-fixed-effects coefficients and the Anderson stepdown $q$-values (computed jointly within each domain to control the false-discovery rate across the set of correlated components).
