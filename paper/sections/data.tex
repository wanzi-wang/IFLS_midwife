\section{Data} \label{sec:data}

\subsection{Indonesia Family Life Survey}

The analysis draws on all five waves of the Indonesia Family Life Survey (IFLS), the household panel fielded in 1993, 1997, 2000, 2007, and 2014. The wave-1 sample is representative of roughly 83 percent of the Indonesian population and covers \num{30000} individuals across 13 of the country's 27 provinces \autocite{strauss2016}. Attrition over the long observation window is moderate and not correlated with the variables that predict adult outcomes: 94 percent of wave-1 households were reinterviewed in wave 2, and the first three waves show no systematic relationship between attrition and baseline observables \autocite{thomas2000}. In the village-midwife setting specifically, \textcite{ahsan2021} find that 23 percent attrition over thirty years does not threaten identification; the present sample shows 21 percent attrition since wave 1, uncorrelated with the timing of village-midwife arrival.

The data construction involves three IFLS modules. The Service Availability Roster (SAR), administered to community informants in waves 2--5, records the type, opening year, and operating status of every health facility serving each community. The community-government (PKK) module independently asks the village leader's wife (who heads the local PKK women's organization) whether a midwife is present and, if so, in what year she first arrived; the two sources yield parallel measures of program rollout, the construction and reconciliation of which are described in Section~\ref{sec:empirical}. Individual demographic and outcome data come from the IFLS household questionnaire.

\subsection{Sample construction}

Treatment is assigned at the level of the individual's community of birth, which IFLS does not record directly. The analysis therefore retains three alternative reconstructions of the birthplace identifier and compares results across them to assess robustness: (i)~a non-mover variant, following \textcite{cas2012} and \textcite{ahsan2021}, that restricts to children whose own community identifier is unchanged across all waves in which they appear; (ii)~a preceding-wave variant that assigns the mother's community at the wave nearest preceding the child's birth; and (iii)~a mother-never-mover variant that restricts on the mother's residential history rather than the child's. The three variants yield samples of \NPrimary, roughly \num{10600}, and \num{5638} individuals. The main analysis uses the non-mover variant because it guarantees a clean birthplace identifier; the cost is dropping children whose families moved across waves. The preceding-wave and mother-never-mover variants appear in the robustness columns of Table~\ref{tab:robustness}.

The main sample contains \NPrimary~non-mover individuals born in 1984--1999, aged 15--30 in 2014, who are alive at wave~5, and distributed across \NVillages~communities. The non-mover restriction is consequential because internal migration was substantial in Indonesia over the relevant period: rural-to-urban migration rose from 17 to 31 percent of the population between 1971 and 1990 \autocite{alatas1993}. Restricting to non-movers selects a sample on which the birthplace identifier is unambiguous; the preceding-wave and mother-never-mover columns of Table~\ref{tab:robustness} test whether the qualitative findings survive on samples that admit internal migration.

\subsection{Outcome measures}

The analysis collapses outcomes into four categories. Each is summarized by a single inverse-covariance-weighted composite index following \textcite{anderson2008}, with raw items retained for sensitivity analysis. By convention, signs are oriented so that higher values of each index indicate a better outcome.

\paragraph{Health.} The health index combines six measurements in wave-5: standing height in centimeters, an indicator for not being underweight (BMI $\geq 18.5$), log-transformed peak expiratory flow, an indicator for not having hypertension, a retrospective indicator for good early-life health, and a self-rated general health rating (re-coded so higher is better).

\paragraph{Cognition.} The cognition index combines four wave-5 adult cognitive measures: the Item Response Theory (IRT)-scored adaptive number-series score, the serial-7 subtraction count, and immediate and delayed word-recall counts. The adult IRT score covers the entire 1984--1999 cohort; an alternative child Raven score is administered only to respondents aged 7--14 at wave~5 and enters only the robustness analysis in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.

\paragraph{Depression.} The depression index is the standardized sum of the ten CESD-10 items administered in the IFLS wave-5 adult module, sign-flipped so that higher values mean fewer depressive symptoms.

\paragraph{Socioemotional traits.} The socioemotional index averages five trait scores constructed from the fifteen Big-Five Inventory items in the IFLS wave-5 adult module: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism (sign-flipped, so that higher values indicate greater emotional stability). Each trait score is standardized within the wave-5 sample.
