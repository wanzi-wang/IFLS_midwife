\section{Empirical strategy} \label{sec:empirical}

\subsection{Methodology}

The Village Midwife Program rolled out across Indonesian communities in different years between 1989 and the late 1990s, generating a staggered-treatment-timing setting. The standard pooled two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) estimator is biased in this setting under treatment-effect heterogeneity: \textcite{goodmanbacon2021} shows that the pooled coefficient is a weighted average of two-by-two comparisons that include already-treated units in the effective control group for later-treated ones, and that some of the implicit weights can be negative. The bias is largest precisely when the treatment effect varies across cohorts or across event times, both of which are central features of an early-life intervention whose effect depends on the developmental window during which exposure occurs.

The main analysis therefore uses the heterogeneity-robust estimator of \textcite{callawaysantanna2021}, hereafter CS, as the main specification. The CS estimator computes group-time average treatment effects $ATT(g, t)$ separately for each cohort $g$ (indexed by the year the village midwife arrived in their community) and each event time $t$, using never-treated communities as the control group. The reported coefficient is the average of $ATT(g, t)$ across event times $0$--$3$, weighted by the number of treated units at each event time. This recovers the average effect of program exposure on the human-capital index for the cohorts exposed in utero or aged 0--3 at midwife arrival, without contamination from already-treated cohorts being used as controls. The pooled TWFE specification is reported as a baseline comparator, and the alternative \textcite{dechaisemartin2021} estimator is reported as a sensitivity check on the choice of aggregation within the heterogeneity-robust family.

\subsection{Specification}

Let $i$ index individuals, $c$ their community of birth, $b$ their year of birth, and $w(i)$ the IFLS wave in which $i$ first appears in the survey panel. The baseline specification for each outcome index $Y_{ic}$ is \begin{equation} Y_{ic} \;=\; \beta\, E_{ic} \;+\; X_{ic}'\theta \;+\; \eta_c \;+\; \gamma_b \;+\; \zeta_{w(i)} \;+\; \varepsilon_{ic}, \label{eq:twfe} \end{equation} where $E_{ic}$ equals one if individual $i$ was in utero or aged 0--3 at the time the village midwife arrived in community $c$; $X_{ic}$ collects pre-determined controls (sex, mother's years of schooling, mother's age at birth); $\eta_c$, $\gamma_b$, and $\zeta_{w(i)}$ are community-of-birth, birth-year, and survey-wave fixed effects; and standard errors are clustered at the community-of-birth level. The coefficient $\beta$ is the average effect on the adult outcome of having been exposed to the program during the developmental window the fetal-origins literature has identified as the most consequential for long-run human capital formation \autocite{barker1995}; the age-three cutoff is taken from that literature rather than estimated from the data. The community and birth-year fixed effects together absorb all time-invariant village characteristics (baseline poverty, remoteness, ethnic composition, geographic isolation) and all cohort-level shocks common across communities (the AFC, national curriculum changes, period-specific aggregate conditions); the survey-wave fixed effect $\zeta_{w(i)}$ absorbs systematic differences across the IFLS waves at which baseline records were collected for different individuals (W1 in 1993, W2 in 1997, W3 in 2000), so that $\beta$ is identified from within-community variation in exposure across adjacent cohorts whose baseline information was collected in the same survey wave. The identification follows \textcite{duflo2001} for the natural-experiment design and \textcite{ahsan2021} for the program-exposure parameterization.

\subsection{Threats to identification}

This empirical strategy meets two concerns, which shape the choice of estimator. The first is the non-random placement of the program across communities. Provincial health departments prioritized communities with the weakest existing maternal and child health infrastructure when allocating midwife graduates \autocite{frankenberg2005,gani1996,makowiecka2008}, so the year a community received a village midwife is mechanically correlated with village-level attributes that themselves affect child outcomes through channels other than the program. The community fixed effect $\eta_c$ in equation~\eqref{eq:twfe} absorbs these attributes only to the extent that they are time-invariant during the cohort window; the within-mother specification developed in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} relaxes the time-invariance restriction by identifying $\beta$ from sibling differences in exposure within a single household.

The second concern is parallel trends. Pre-trends are tested with a cohort-aggregated event-study specification, \begin{equation} Y_{ic} \;=\; \sum_{e \neq -1} \delta_e \,\mathbf{1}\{b_i - g_c = e\} \;+\; \eta_c \;+\; \gamma_b \;+\; u_{ic}, \label{eq:sunab} \end{equation} with event time $-1$ as the omitted reference. Coefficients are displayed in Figure~\ref{fig:event_study} and reported numerically in Table~\ref{tab:pretrend}; a joint Wald test of $\delta_{-4} = \delta_{-2} = 0$ provides the parallel-trends diagnostic. Event-time $-3$ is excluded because only a thin set of cohort-village cells identifies that coefficient (kids born in or before $\text{start}_c - 4$ in each treated community) and the resulting estimate is noisy rather than informative.

\subsection{Within-mother specification}

The central robustness in this paper is a within-mother specification that absorbs every time-invariant village characteristic, observed or unobserved, by identifying $\beta$ from sibling differences in exposure within a single household. The specification is \begin{equation} Y_{ic} \;=\; \beta\, E_{ic} \;+\; X_{ic}'\theta \;+\; \alpha_{m(i)} \;+\; \gamma_b \;+\; \zeta_{w(i)} \;+\; \varepsilon_{ic}, \label{eq:sibfe} \end{equation} where $\alpha_{m(i)}$ is a fixed effect for the mother of individual $i$, $\gamma_b$ and $\zeta_{w(i)}$ are birth-year and survey-wave fixed effects as in equation~\eqref{eq:twfe}, and $X_{ic}$ additionally includes birth order and mother's age at birth, on which siblings vary even within a single mother. Identification comes from differential exposure across siblings born to the same mother before and after the midwife arrived in their community. The analysis sample contains \num{2796} mothers with at least two children in cohorts 1984--1999 and \num{1153} with three or more, which provides the power to estimate equation~\eqref{eq:sibfe}. A fertility response to the program itself would threaten the design, and the corresponding fertility-selection check is reported in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.

\subsection{Identifying assumptions}

Identification of equation~\eqref{eq:twfe} requires parallel trends: in the absence of the program, treated and control cohorts within a community would have evolved similarly. The within-mother specification weakens this to the assumption that, conditional on the mother and on observable birth-order and age-at-birth controls, sibling differences in exposure are exogenous to the outcome through channels other than the program. The \textcite{callawaysantanna2021} estimator requires parallel trends only between each cohort group and the never-treated, with no extrapolation across cohort groups. SUTVA requires that treatment of one community not affect outcomes in another. \textcite{ahsan2021} discuss this restriction in the context of midwife mobility across village boundaries; any violation plausibly biases estimates toward zero, and this paper does not separately correct for the bias.
