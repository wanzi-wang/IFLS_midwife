\section{Empirical strategy} \label{sec:empirical}

\subsection{Specification}

Let $i$ index individuals, $c$ their community of birth, and $b$ their year of birth. The baseline specification for each outcome index $Y_{ic}$ is \begin{equation} Y_{ic} \;=\; \beta\, E_{ic} \;+\; X_{ic}'\theta \;+\; \eta_c \;+\; \gamma_b \;+\; \varepsilon_{ic}, \label{eq:twfe} \end{equation} where $E_{ic}$ is an indicator equal to one if individual $i$ was in utero or aged 0--3 at the time the village midwife arrived in community $c$; $X_{ic}$ is a vector of pre-determined controls (sex, mother's years of schooling, mother's age at birth); $\eta_c$ and $\gamma_b$ are community-of-birth and birth-year fixed effects; and standard errors are clustered at the community-of-birth level. The coefficient $\beta$ is the parameter of interest: the average effect on the adult outcome of having been exposed to the program during the developmental window that the fetal-origins literature has identified as the most consequential for long-run human capital formation \autocite{barker1995}. The age-three threshold is taken from that literature rather than estimated from the data.

The specification follows the natural-experiment architecture of \textcite{duflo2001} and the program-exposure architecture of \textcite{ahsan2021}. The community and birth-year fixed effects together absorb all time-invariant village characteristics (baseline poverty, remoteness, ethnic composition, geographic isolation) and all cohort-level shocks that are common across communities (the national-level AFC shock, national curriculum changes, period-specific aggregate conditions). The coefficient $\beta$ is therefore identified from within-village variation in exposure across adjacent cohorts.

\subsection{Threats to identification}

Two standard concerns arise in this setting. The first is the non-random placement of the program across communities. Provincial health departments prioritised communities with the weakest existing maternal and child health infrastructure when allocating midwife graduates \autocite{frankenberg2005,gani1996,makowiecka2008}, so the year a community received a village midwife is mechanically correlated with village-level attributes that themselves affect child outcomes through channels other than the program itself. Equation~\eqref{eq:twfe}'s community fixed effect $\eta_c$ absorbs these attributes to the extent that they are time-invariant during the cohort window; the within-mother specification in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} absorbs them without the time-invariance restriction by identifying $\beta$ from sibling differences in exposure within a single household.

The second concern is that the canonical two-way fixed-effects estimator with staggered treatment timing is known to be biased in the presence of heterogeneous treatment effects, because already-treated units enter the effective control group for later-treated ones \autocite{goodmanbacon2021}. The main results therefore also report the heterogeneity-robust estimator of \textcite{callawaysantanna2021}, which uses never-treated communities as the control group, and the alternative \textcite{dechaisemartin2021} estimator; both are reported alongside the TWFE column so the reader can see how much of the headline number reflects staggered-rollout biases rather than the program.

Pre-trends are tested with a cohort-aggregated event-study specification, \begin{equation} Y_{ic} \;=\; \sum_{e \neq -1} \delta_e \,\mathbf{1}\{b_i - g_c = e\} \;+\; \eta_c \;+\; \gamma_b \;+\; u_{ic}, \label{eq:sunab} \end{equation} with $-1$ as the omitted reference event time. The coefficients are displayed in Figure~\ref{fig:event_study} and reported numerically in Table~\ref{tab:pretrend}; a joint Wald test of $\delta_{-4} = \delta_{-3} = \delta_{-2} = 0$ provides the parallel-trends diagnostic.

\subsection{Within-mother specification}

The central robustness in this paper is a within-mother specification that absorbs every time-invariant village characteristic, observable or unobservable, by identifying $\beta$ from sibling differences in exposure within a single household. The specification is \begin{equation} Y_{ic} \;=\; \beta\, E_{ic} \;+\; X_{ic}'\theta \;+\; \alpha_{m(i)} \;+\; \gamma_b \;+\; \varepsilon_{ic}, \label{eq:sibfe} \end{equation} where $\alpha_{m(i)}$ is a fixed effect for the mother of individual $i$ and $X_{ic}$ additionally includes birth order and mother's age at birth (siblings differ on these even within mother). Identification comes from differential exposure across siblings born to the same mother before versus after the midwife's arrival in their community. The analysis sample contains \num{2796} mothers with at least two children in cohorts 1984--1999 and \num{1153} mothers with three or more, which provides the power to estimate equation~\eqref{eq:sibfe}. Fertility responses to the program itself would threaten this design, and a fertility-selection check is reported in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.

\subsection{Identifying assumptions}

Identification of equation~\eqref{eq:twfe} requires parallel trends: in the absence of the program, treated and control cohorts within a community would have evolved similarly. The within-mother specification weakens this to the assumption that, conditional on the mother and on the observable birth-order and age-at-birth controls, sibling differences in exposure are exogenous to the outcome through channels other than the program itself. The \textcite{callawaysantanna2021} estimator requires parallel trends only between each cohort group and the never-treated, with no extrapolation across cohort groups. SUTVA requires that treatment of one community does not affect outcomes in another, a restriction that \textcite{ahsan2021} discuss in the context of midwife mobility across village boundaries and that plausibly biases estimates toward zero in a way this paper does not separately correct.
