\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why are the main effects so small?}

The headline TWFE estimates in Table~\ref{tab:main_results} are
substantively smaller than what the predecessor DEPII analysis and
\textcite{ahsan2021} report on similar data. Three explanations are
worth considering.

\paragraph{Placement bias suppresses the estimates.}
The within-mother specification produces a depression-index estimate
nearly twice the size of the TWFE estimate, and the corresponding
female health-index estimate is also somewhat larger under sibling FE.
This is the pattern that would be expected if the program were placed
in communities that, absent the program, would have produced cohorts
with worse adult health and mental-health outcomes. The TWFE estimator
absorbs the cross-community gap into the community fixed effect but
cannot separate the program's positive effect from the underlying
village vulnerability that selected the village into early treatment.
Sibling FE removes this confound by construction. The fact that
sibling-FE moves the depression estimate up while leaving cognition
near zero is consistent with the program operating primarily through
mental-health and physical-health channels rather than through
cognition.

\paragraph{The fluid--crystallized cognitive ageing pattern.}
\textcite{tuckerdrob2022} document that fluid cognitive abilities---the
construct measured by IRT-scored adaptive number-series tasks of the
\texttt{w\_abil} kind---begin to decline from early adulthood onward,
while crystallized abilities continue to accumulate. The mean age of
the analysis sample at wave~5 is~22, which is on the threshold of the
fluid-ability decline. Any cognitive premium from early-life nutrition
or care may therefore be partly attenuated by age-related change in the
underlying cognitive trait by the time it is measured. This
interpretation is consistent with the predecessor DEPII analysis's
finding that no detectable cognitive effect appeared on the child
Raven score either, on the (younger but smaller) Book~EK subsample.

\paragraph{Rollout measurement error.}
The SAR and PKK rollout-year measures disagree on roughly 83 percent of
communities for which both are recorded; the Pearson correlation
between the two measures is essentially zero (Appendix
\ref{app:rollout}). This level of disagreement is enormous in
absolute terms and is a source of classical measurement error in the
treatment variable that attenuates the headline estimates toward zero
in the manner familiar from Cas (2012)'s discussion. The practical
implication is that any single-source rollout estimate is
under-powered relative to the true variation in program access; the
robustness column~R4 swap to the PKK measure is the relevant
sensitivity check, and it does not produce systematically larger
estimates.

\subsection{The gender pattern}

The concentration of health-index gains among girls is the empirical
finding from the predecessor DEPII analysis that survives most cleanly
into the present design. Two channels are plausible. First, midwives'
nutrition-counseling and supplementation services may have larger
marginal returns for girls in households that allocated nutritional
resources preferentially to boys at baseline; the effect of leveling
the nutrition gradient would mechanically be larger for the
disadvantaged group. Second, the antenatal-care services provided by
midwives include screening for sex-specific complications of
pregnancy, and may have differentially reduced the risk of low-birth-
weight outcomes for female infants if the underlying baseline rate of
inadequate prenatal care was higher for pregnancies expected to
produce daughters. Both interpretations are speculative; testing them
directly would require restricted IFLS data on contemporaneous
household-level resource allocation that the present analysis does not
have.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Sample restriction.}
The headline analysis restricts on neither migration history nor
cohort. The predecessor DEPII analysis restricted on the non-migrant
sample, which has the advantage of cleanly identified birthplace at
the cost of dropping roughly half the cohort. Internal migration in
Indonesia rose substantially across the relevant decades
\autocite{alatas1993}, so the restriction is not innocuous. The
preceding-wave birthplace variant adopted here is a compromise that
infers birthplace from the mother's commune at the wave nearest
preceding the child's birth; it is necessarily noisier than the
non-migrant restriction for births several years before wave~1 (1993).
The robustness columns R5 and R6 in Table~\ref{tab:robustness} report
estimates on the alternative birthplace variants; the qualitative
patterns survive but the smaller samples produce wider confidence
intervals.

\paragraph{Spillovers and midwife mobility.}
The empirical strategy assumes the standard SUTVA condition: that
treatment of one community does not affect outcomes in another. In
practice midwives may treat patients from neighboring communities,
and may move between communities over the course of their careers
\autocite{jointcommittee2013}. \textcite{ahsan2021} discuss this issue
in the context of the same data and conclude that across-community
spillovers are likely to bias estimates toward zero rather than away
from zero, since untreated communities adjacent to treated ones would
appear less ``untreated'' than the SAR records suggest. The present
analysis does not separately model spillovers; the implication, if
\textcite{ahsan2021} are correct about the direction, is that the
headline estimates are conservative.

\paragraph{Pre-trend violations.}
The Sun--Abraham pre-trend test rejects parallel trends for three of
four outcomes. The visual inspection of Figure~\ref{fig:event_study}
suggests the rejection is driven by noisy pre-period coefficients
straddling zero rather than by a systematic pre-period trend in any
one direction, but a strict reading of the parallel-trends assumption
is rejected. The fact that the within-mother estimates do not
mechanically inherit this violation---because they remove all
time-invariant village confounders rather than relying on a parallel
counterfactual cohort trend---is the principal reason the
sibling-FE depression result deserves particular weight.

\paragraph{Oster fragility.}
The $\delta^*$ values reported in Table~\ref{tab:robustness} are all
below one. The headline TWFE estimates are fragile in the technical
sense: even modest selection on unobservables would erase them. This
is the central caveat of the paper. The estimates that survive the
robustness suite---the within-mother depression result, the female
health result---survive because the design itself addresses the
unobservable-selection concern, not because the coefficients are
robust to weakening of the identifying assumptions.

\subsection{Extensions}

The most informative extension would use restricted IFLS data to
construct a non-restricted birthplace measure, which would obviate
both the migration-restriction trade-off and the
preceding-wave noise documented above. A second extension would
exploit the AFC-induced contraction in midwife stocks during the
1997--2000 window as a second natural experiment in program access;
unfortunately the SAR's exit-year fields are not consistently
filled across waves, so the contraction can only be measured at the
3-year resolution of PKK presence indicators, which yields too few
events to support a precise analysis. A third extension would
estimate the program's effect on intermediate outcomes---measured
prenatal care use, immunization coverage, breastfeeding behavior---to
tighten the mechanism story behind the depression and health results.
\textcite{frankenberg2005} and \textcite{triyana2016} both report
relevant intermediate-outcome estimates that could be combined with
the present long-run estimates to construct a fuller mechanism
analysis.
