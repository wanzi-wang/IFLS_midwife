\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpreting the magnitudes}

The \CoefHealthCS~standard-deviation effect on the adult physical-health index translates into a change on the order of a two-centimetre shift in adult height, since adult height is the largest weight in the Anderson health index and its analysis-sample dispersion is roughly eight centimetres. The effect is in the range the early-life-environments literature has documented for other public-health interventions with long-horizon targets \autocite{duflo2001,maccini2009,majid2015,ahsan2021} and meaningfully larger than the short-run effects typically reported for cash-transfer programs in comparable low-income settings \autocite{premand2022}.

The event-study coefficients trace out the same pattern on a cohort-by-cohort basis. Health-index effects emerge during the in-utero and 0--3 event-time windows, persist through event-time $+3$, and decay thereafter, consistent with the critical-periods interpretation advanced by \textcite{barker1995}, \textcite{duflo2001}, and \textcite{maccini2009}: the prenatal and early-postnatal windows are the periods during which the developmental system is most plastic and during which nutrition, infection control, and preventive care have the largest cumulative effects on long-run outcomes.

\subsection{Why cognition, depression, and socioemotional outcomes are less responsive}

The physical-health effect sits against null estimates on cognition and depressive symptoms and a negative, marginally significant estimate on the socioemotional index. Four non-mutually-exclusive explanations are consistent with this pattern and help to discipline its policy interpretation.

\paragraph{The midwife's service bundle is narrow relative to the outcome set.} A village midwife delivered prenatal monitoring, skilled attendance at birth, postnatal visits, family planning, nutrition counselling, and iron and vitamin~A supplementation. This bundle is engineered to prevent infant and maternal mortality and to improve birth and early-childhood anthropometric outcomes, through which it feeds into the physical-health index a generation later. It is not engineered to deliver the parenting-skill training, early-childhood stimulation, preschool learning, or household mental-health services that the developmental literature identifies as proximate inputs into cognitive and socioemotional development \autocite{webb2024,sorrenti2025}. On this reading the null estimates on cognition and socioemotional traits are not a weakness of the program, but a mark of its specificity: a program that does one thing well produces effects on the margin it targeted.

\paragraph{The 1984--1999 cohort window mixes full, partial, and no exposure.} The sample follows every child born between 1984 and 1999 in a village that either received a midwife during the rollout or never did. A child born in 1984 in a 1995-rollout village was eleven at program arrival and receives no "age 0--3" exposure; a child born in 1997 in the same village was exposed in utero. The cohort-specific treatment effects CS recovers average across this spectrum, and the broader the exposure variation, the wider the confidence interval on margins for which the underlying effect is smaller or more heterogeneous. A narrower cohort window centred on the cohorts that received the highest-intensity exposure would sharpen the cognition and mental-health estimates, at the cost of statistical power.

\paragraph{Adult cognition and mental-health scales carry substantial measurement noise.} The cognition index is built from the Raven-style number-series, immediate and delayed word recall, and serial-seven subtractions administered at wave~5 (2014). These scales are noisy proxies for the underlying cognitive construct, particularly among respondents aged 15--30 whose fluid abilities are still fluctuating with age \autocite{tuckerdrob2022}. The CESD-10 depression score and the Big-Five socioemotional inventory similarly depend on a single self-report occasion. A larger early-life intervention could produce detectable effects on adult physical health and still fail to register on these scales at the sample size the present panel provides.

\paragraph{Adult mental health and personality are shaped by contemporaneous factors.} Physical adult health is substantially pinned down by nutrition and infection exposure in the early-life window. Adult depression and personality at age 15--30 reflect contemporaneous employment, relationship quality, schooling trajectory, and the household environment the respondent grew up in, none of which a pre-birth midwife program is designed to directly change. The positive but imprecise cognition and depression estimates should therefore be read as the residual of whatever channel operates from early-life health through to these adult outcomes, not as the direct effect the program was intended to produce.

\paragraph{Policy implication.} For policy, the pattern of findings implies that a national maternal and neonatal health program centred on skilled care at birth can deliver measurable adult-physical-health returns at the population level, a generation later, and the mechanism evidence points to skilled birth attendance as the plausible proximate channel. It also implies that advancing cognitive, mental-health, and socioemotional outcomes at the same scale would require programs designed to reach those margins directly, for example early-childhood-development services, preschool provision, or parenting interventions, layered on top of the clinical bundle the village midwife delivers rather than expected to flow automatically from it.

\subsection{The gender split}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results_by_sex} reports the TWFE baseline estimated separately on male and female subsamples. None of the four index coefficients on either subsample reaches conventional significance; the only marginally significant cell is a ten-percent-level negative estimate on the socioemotional index for boys. The sign pattern on the health index, a small positive estimate for boys and a small negative estimate for girls, points in the opposite direction from the gender-specific pattern \textcite{frankenberg2005} documented on earlier IFLS waves and a non-migrant subsample. The disagreement is a feature of the analysis-sample restriction rather than a contradiction of the earlier literature: the non-migrant cohort followed into wave~5 is both younger and more rural than the pre-program cohort Frankenberg studied, and on this sample the by-sex split does not identify a gender-specific effect at the precision the present observations permit.

\subsection{The mechanism story}

The mechanism analysis in Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms} locates the most plausible channel through which the program's reduced-form health effect operates. Among the three mother-reported mediators extracted from the pregnancy-history module, skilled birth attendance is the only mediator with meaningful baseline variation for a marginal intervention to move; the other two (any prenatal check-up and breastfeeding initiation) have near-ceiling baseline means above 0.93 and leave no room for a marginal program to shift the mean. The first-stage estimate for skilled birth attendance is seven percentage points, the largest in the mediator set both in absolute terms and relative to baseline, though imprecise on its own (SE 0.052, $p > 0.10$). The Baron--Kenny decomposition attributes about twelve percent of the mediator-sub-sample reduced-form health effect to variation in skilled birth attendance. This is a share of a small number rather than of the \CoefHealthCS~SD effect on the full non-migrant sample; the right reading is that skilled birth attendance is a plausible channel consistent with the pregnancy-history data, not a channel whose quantitative share of the adult-health effect is pinned down.

\subsection{Within-mother evidence and between-estimator coherence}

The sibling-fixed-effects estimates in Table~\ref{tab:sibling_fe} add a qualifier to the headline rather than ratifying it. On the depression index the within-mother estimate (\CoefDepSibFE~SD, SE \SEDepSibFE) is larger than the pooled TWFE benchmark, consistent with the program lifting adult mental health through channels absorbed by a cross-sectional community fixed effect but visible across siblings within a family. On the health index the within-mother estimate is negative on all three sibling-FE specifications, at magnitudes between $-0.19$ and $-0.33$ standard deviations, where the corresponding CS estimate is $+0.31$. The two estimators identify the treatment effect off different variation, so a divergence between them is informative rather than contradictory: if the program's effect on adult health operates primarily through between-family channels, the within-mother estimator would be attenuated or reverse sign relative to the between-cohort effect. A fertility-selection response could also drive the divergence, but the within-community check in Table~\ref{tab:fertility} shows this effect is small. The paper reports both estimators.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Sample restriction.} The non-migrant restriction guarantees a cleanly-identified birthplace but halves the available sample relative to the preceding-wave variant. Internal migration rose substantially in Indonesia across the relevant decades, with rural-to-urban migration climbing from 17 to 31~percent of the population between 1971 and 1990 \autocite{alatas1993}. The robustness columns R5 and R6 in Table~\ref{tab:robustness} confirm that the qualitative patterns survive on alternative birthplace variants but with wider confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Spillovers and midwife mobility.} The identification strategy assumes the standard SUTVA condition: treatment of one community does not affect outcomes in another. In practice midwives may treat patients from neighbouring communities and may move between communities over their careers \autocite{jointcommittee2013}. \textcite{ahsan2021} discuss this issue on the same data and conclude that across-community spillovers would bias estimates toward zero because untreated communities adjacent to treated ones would appear less untreated than the SAR records suggest.

\paragraph{Pre-trend evidence and PTA sensitivity.} The CS event study in Figure~\ref{fig:event_study} shows a two-sigma negative coefficient at event-time $-3$ on the health index. The \textcite{rambachanroth2023} sensitivity analysis reported in Section~\ref{sec:honestdid} quantifies the consequences for the headline. On the baseline pre-period specification the robust confidence set admits zero for any substantive $\bar{M}$, so on a strict reading the headline health effect rests on parallel trends holding close to exactly. On a trimmed pre-period specification that drops the thin-cell $t=-3$ extreme-lead coefficient, the robust CI remains close to the original CS interval across a wide range of $\bar{M}$, with the point estimate staying near $0.31$~SD. The truth lies somewhere between these readings, and the paper is transparent about which parts of the headline survive which reading.

\subsection{Extensions}

A non-restricted birthplace measure built from restricted IFLS data would resolve the migration trade-off without relying on the preceding-wave approximation. A richer mechanism analysis that exploits the contraction in midwife stocks induced by the Asian Financial Crisis as a second natural experiment could sharpen identification of which component of the service bundle is doing the work; the SAR's exit-year fields are not consistently filled across waves, so the contraction can at present only be measured at the three-year resolution of PKK presence indicators, which yields too few events to support a precise analysis. Finally, the mechanism evidence could be strengthened with a formal mediation-inference approach in the \textcite{imai2010} framework, which would require additional assumptions the present data cannot directly test.
