\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpreting the magnitudes}

The \CoefHealthCS~standard-deviation effect on the adult physical-health index translates into a change on the order of a two-centimeter shift in adult height, since adult height is the largest weight in the Anderson health index and its analysis-sample dispersion is roughly eight centimeters. The effect is in the range the early-life-environments literature has documented for other public-health interventions with long-horizon targets \autocite{duflo2001,maccini2009,majid2015,ahsan2021} and meaningfully larger than the short-run effects typically reported for cash-transfer programs in comparable low-income settings \autocite{premand2022}.

The event-study coefficients trace out the same pattern on a cohort-by-cohort basis. Health-index effects emerge during the in-utero and 0--3 event-time windows, persist through event-time $+3$, and decay thereafter, consistent with the critical-periods interpretation advanced by \textcite{barker1995}, \textcite{duflo2001}, and \textcite{maccini2009}: the prenatal and early-postnatal windows are the periods during which the developmental system is most plastic and during which nutrition, infection control, and preventive care have the largest cumulative effects on long-run outcomes.

\subsection{Why depression and socioemotional outcomes are less responsive}

The physical-health and cognition effects sit against null estimates on depressive symptoms (\CoefDepCS~SD) and a small negative estimate on the socioemotional index that does not reach statistical significance. Three explanations are consistent with this pattern and help to discipline its policy interpretation.

\paragraph{The midwife's service bundle is narrow relative to the outcome set.} A village midwife delivered prenatal monitoring, skilled attendance at birth, postnatal visits, family planning, nutrition counseling, and iron and vitamin~A supplementation. This bundle is engineered to prevent infant and maternal mortality and to improve birth and early-childhood nutritional and infection-control outcomes, through which it can feed into both adult physical health and adult cognition a generation later \autocite{almondcurrie2011, currievogl2013}. It is not engineered to deliver the parenting-skill training, household mental-health services, or socioemotional-development programs that the developmental literature identifies as proximate inputs into adult mental health and personality \autocite{webb2024,sorrenti2025}. On this reading the null estimates on depression and socioemotional outcomes are not a weakness of the program, but a mark of its specificity: a program that does one thing well produces effects on the margins it targeted.

\paragraph{Adult mental-health and personality scales carry substantial measurement noise.} The CESD-10 depression score and the Big-Five socioemotional inventory each depend on a single self-report occasion at wave~5 (2014), administered to respondents aged 15--30 whose mental-health and personality profiles are still actively forming. A larger early-life intervention could produce detectable effects on adult physical health and cognition and still fail to register on these scales at the sample size the present panel provides.

\paragraph{Adult mental health and personality are shaped by contemporaneous factors.} Adult depression and personality at age 15--30 reflect contemporaneous employment, relationship quality, schooling trajectory, and the household environment the respondent grew up in, none of which a pre-birth midwife program is designed to directly change. The null estimates on these two margins should therefore be read as the residual of whatever channel operates from early-life health through to these adult outcomes, not as the direct effect the program was intended to produce.

\paragraph{Policy implication.} For policy, the pattern of findings implies that a national maternal and neonatal health program centered on skilled care at birth can deliver measurable adult-physical-health and cognition returns at the population level, a generation later, with skilled birth attendance the plausible proximate channel. It also implies that advancing depression and socioemotional outcomes at the same scale would require programs designed to reach those margins directly, for example mental-health services, parenting interventions, or community psychosocial supports, layered on top of the clinical bundle the village midwife delivers rather than expected to flow automatically from it.

\subsection{The gender split}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results_by_sex} reports the TWFE baseline estimated separately on male and female subsamples. The socioemotional-index estimate is significantly negative on boys ($-0.306$, SE $0.143$, $p<0.05$) and indistinguishable from zero on girls; none of the other index coefficients on either subsample reaches statistical significance. The sign pattern on the health index, a small positive estimate for boys ($+0.023$) and a moderate negative estimate for girls ($-0.152$), points in the opposite direction from the gender-specific pattern \textcite{frankenberg2005} documented on earlier IFLS waves and a non-migrant subsample. The disagreement is a feature of the analysis-sample restriction rather than a contradiction of the earlier literature: the non-migrant cohort followed into wave~5 is both younger and more rural than the pre-program cohort Frankenberg studied, and on this sample the by-sex split does not identify a gender-specific health effect at the precision the present observations permit. The lone significant cell, the negative socioemotional coefficient on boys, is one of eight subsample-by-outcome tests and would not survive a simple multiple-comparison adjustment.

\subsection{The mechanism explanation}

The mechanism analysis in Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms} narrows the candidate channels through which the program's reduced-form health effect operates to a single program-specific margin. Among the five mother-reported mediators in the pregnancy-history module, only the village-midwife-specific delivery-attendant indicator moves with in-utero program exposure: the first-stage point estimate is \num{0.045} (SE \num{0.026}, $p \approx 0.08$), a roughly $4.5$ percentage-point increase on a baseline mean of $8.3$ percent. The aggregate any-skilled-attendant indicator does not move (\num{0.001}, SE \num{0.032}), the prenatal-care indicator is near-ceiling (sample mean \num{0.94}) and unmoved, and neither the exclusive-breastfeeding indicator nor recorded birth weight responds. Together these estimates imply that the program shifted the composition of skilled deliveries toward village midwives without expanding the overall rate of skilled care, consistent with substitution into village-midwife attendance from traditional birth attendants and from private midwives in the same community.

\subsection{Within-mother evidence}

The sibling-fixed-effects estimates in Table~\ref{tab:sibling_fe} are reported for transparency rather than as a corroborating estimator. None of the within-mother point estimates on the four indices is individually distinguishable from zero, and the cluster-robust standard errors are roughly twice as wide as those on the cross-sectional regressions because the within-mother contrast discards the cross-community variation that delivers most of the program's identifying signal. On the health index in particular the within-mother point estimates run between $-0.16$ and $-0.26$ standard deviations against the $+\CoefHealthCS$~SD CS estimate, but each cell has $|t| < 1.6$ and the gap is best read as a precision collapse on a much smaller subsample rather than as evidence of channel divergence between within-family and cross-cohort comparisons. The within-community fertility-selection check in Table~\ref{tab:fertility} confirms that mothers did not change their fertility timing in response to the midwife's arrival, so the within-mother sample is not contaminated by selective birth-timing.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Sample restriction.} The non-migrant restriction guarantees a cleanly-identified birthplace but halves the available sample relative to the preceding-wave variant. Internal migration rose substantially in Indonesia across the relevant decades, with rural-to-urban migration climbing from 17 to 31~percent of the population between 1971 and 1990 \autocite{alatas1993}. The robustness columns R5 and R6 in Table~\ref{tab:robustness} confirm that the qualitative patterns survive on alternative birthplace variants but with wider confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Spillovers and midwife mobility.} The identification strategy assumes the standard SUTVA condition: treatment of one community does not affect outcomes in another. In practice midwives may treat patients from neighbouring communities and may move between communities over their careers \autocite{jointcommittee2013}. \textcite{ahsan2021} discuss this issue on the same data and conclude that across-community spillovers would bias estimates toward zero because untreated communities adjacent to treated ones would appear less untreated than the SAR records suggest.

\subsection{Extensions}

Three extensions of the present design hinge on data the public IFLS releases do not include. A birthplace measure built from restricted-access community identifiers would resolve the trade-off the paper currently makes between sample size and clean birthplace identification, and would let the CS estimator run on a sample that retains internal migrants without an inferred-birthplace approximation. The contraction in village-midwife postings induced by the Asian Financial Crisis offers a second natural experiment for separating components of the program's service bundle, but exploiting it requires consistently-filled SAR exit-year fields that the public release does not provide; at the three-year resolution of the PKK presence indicators currently available, the contraction yields too few cleanly-identified exits to support a precise analysis. A finer mediator decomposition would draw on trimester-specific antenatal visits, tetanus-toxoid coverage, iron supplementation, and child immunization registers, which are recorded in the IFLS book-4 module only for the late cohort and would, on a longer panel, allow a more granular reading of the service-bundle channel than the village-midwife-attendance margin alone supports.
