\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

The empirical strategy in Section~\ref{sec:empirical} is designed
around the expectation that the headline TWFE estimates will be
fragile under any one identifying assumption. The robustness suite in
this section makes that expectation explicit: each subsection tests
the headline estimates against a perturbation that targets a specific
threat. The within-mother specification is reported first because it
is the headline robustness; the remaining columns are organized as a
single table (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}) for compactness.

\subsection{Sibling fixed effects}

Table~\ref{tab:sibling_fe} reports estimates of equation
\eqref{eq:sibfe}, which adds a mother fixed effect to the baseline
specification. Three subsamples are reported: mothers with at least
two observed children (column~A), the same subsample with an
additional community-of-birth fixed effect (column~B), and mothers
with at least three observed children (column~C). The depression
index, which had a TWFE point estimate of \CoefDepTWFE~SD
(SE \SEDepTWFE) in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, rises to
\CoefDepSibFE~SD (SE \SEDepSibFE) under the simple mother-FE
specification and remains close to that magnitude with the additional
community fixed effect and on the more restrictive sibship subsample.

The interpretation of the rise in the depression-index estimate is
the central substantive point of the robustness analysis. If the
program were operating uniformly across communities and the
TWFE--sibling-FE gap reflected only sampling variation, we would
expect the two estimates to be statistically indistinguishable. The
direction and magnitude of the gap---a near-doubling of the point
estimate, in the direction of larger positive effects---is consistent
with the prior narrative that TWFE was suppressing the program's true
effect on mental health by absorbing the difference between
early-treated communities (poorer, sicker, with more depression in
both treated and control cohorts) and late-treated communities
(better-off, healthier, with less depression). Once the within-mother
comparison removes the time-invariant village confounders that drove
this comparison, the program's effect on adult depression is somewhat
larger, though still imprecise.

The health-index point estimate also moves under sibling FE, but in
the opposite direction: from \CoefHealthTWFE\ to roughly $-0.05$~SD.
Since the headline TWFE estimate is itself indistinguishable from
zero, this movement is best read as further evidence that the headline
sample is uninformative about the health margin in the absence of the
gender-specific decomposition reported in
Table~\ref{tab:main_results_by_sex}.

\subsection{Fertility-selection check}

Sibling fixed effects fail to identify the program's effect if
mothers respond to midwife arrival by changing the timing or
spacing of their births in ways that correlate with child outcomes.
Table~\ref{tab:fertility} reports a within-community check that
asks whether mothers whose reproductive years overlapped with the
midwife's tenure had more observed children in the 1984--1999 window.
The point estimate is positive, but the test is partly mechanical:
younger mothers (whose reproductive years overlap more with the
program) naturally have more births observable in the
1984--1999 window than older mothers (whose reproductive years were
earlier). The test should therefore be interpreted as a weak diagnostic
rather than a sharp test; a cleaner test would require holding mother
age constant, which the within-mother fixed effect already does. Read
in that light, the implication is that any fertility-response
contamination of the sibling-FE estimates is small in magnitude and
does not flip the qualitative conclusion.

\subsection{Other robustness columns}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness} collects the remaining robustness checks.
Column~R1 weights the TWFE specification by the inverse propensity of
early treatment (the predecessor DEPII analysis's principal
correction); the estimates are stable but no more informative than
the unweighted baseline, and the propensity-score model itself is
sensitive to the choice of baseline covariates. Column~R2 adds
cohort-dependent indicators for whether each child's community had a
doctor or clinic at the time of the child's birth---a check on the
``co-rollout'' threat that other facility expansions might be confounded
with the midwife. The estimates are essentially unchanged. Column~R3
replaces the province-by-birth-year fixed effects with province-specific
linear time trends in birth year; this allows different secular trends
across provinces while imposing more structure than the saturated FEs.
Column~R4 swaps the SAR-recorded rollout year for the alternative PKK
measure (which I show in Appendix~\ref{app:rollout} disagrees with the
SAR measure on 83\% of communities); the point estimates change in
sign for some outcomes but remain statistically indistinguishable from
zero. Columns~R5 and~R6 swap the headline ``preceding-wave'' birthplace
variant for the legacy never-mover and mother-never-mover variants
respectively; the smaller samples produce wider confidence intervals
but the qualitative pattern survives.

\paragraph{Oster $\delta^*$.}
Column~R7 reports the \textcite{anderson2008} sensitivity statistic
$\delta^*$, which expresses the ratio of selection on unobservables to
selection on observables that would be required to drive the headline
TWFE estimate to zero, taking $R^2_{\max} = \min(1.3 \cdot R^2_{\text{long}}, 1)$
following the convention in the literature. A $\delta^*$ of one means
that unobservable confounders are exactly as important as observable
ones; values below one mean that even modest selection on
unobservables would erase the estimate. The reported $\delta^*$ values
are all below one (range $0.02$--$0.28$). The headline TWFE estimates
are, by this metric, fragile. This is the most important caveat in the
paper: the estimates that survive the robustness suite---the
within-mother depression result, the female health result---do so
because the design itself addresses the unobservable-selection
concern, not because the coefficients are robust to weakening of the
identifying assumptions.

\subsection{Placebo permutation}

Table~\ref{tab:placebo} reports a within-province placebo test on the
health index. I randomly permute the rollout year within
province-by-cohort cells, refit the TWFE specification, and compare
the placebo coefficient to the actual estimate. Across 200
permutations, 57.5 percent of placebo coefficients exceed the actual
estimate in absolute value. This is consistent with the actual
estimate being indistinguishable from random noise in the
unconditional TWFE specification---which, given the imprecision
documented in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, is the expected result.

\subsection{Alternative cognition outcome}

Table~\ref{tab:child_raven} reports the cognition coefficient using
the child Raven score from IFLS Book~EK---the cognitive measure used
by the predecessor DEPII analysis. The estimate is essentially zero
on a substantially smaller sample, consistent with the
power-related concerns described in Section~\ref{sec:data} that
motivated the switch to the adult IRT score in the headline
specification.
