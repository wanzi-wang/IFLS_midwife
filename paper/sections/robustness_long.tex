\section{Robustness} \label{sec:robustness}

The estimates of Section~\ref{sec:results} are subjected to a series of robustness checks that probe the sensitivity of the findings to alternative sample definitions, alternative measures of program rollout, and alternative identifying assumptions. The principal check is a within-mother specification that identifies the program's effect from sibling differences in exposure timing; the remaining checks are presented in a sensitivity table.

\subsection{Sibling fixed effects}

Table~\ref{tab:sibling_fe} reports estimates of equation \eqref{eq:sibfe}, which replaces the community fixed effect of the baseline specification with a mother fixed effect. Identification comes from differential exposure across siblings born to the same mother before versus after the midwife's arrival in their community. Three subsamples are reported: mothers with at least two observed children in the analysis sample (column~A), the same subsample with a community-of-birth fixed effect additionally layered on top (column~B), and mothers with at least three observed children (column~C).

\begin{table}[!htbp] \centering \caption{Within-mother (sibling-FE) specifications.} \label{tab:sibling_fe} \input{tables/sibling_fe.tex} \begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth} \footnotesize \emph{Notes.} Column~A includes mother and birth-year fixed effects plus controls for birth order and mother's age at birth. Column~B adds community-of-birth fixed effects. Column~C is the same as column~A but restricted to mothers with three or more observed children (833 child-observations). Standard errors clustered at the community-of-birth level. \end{minipage} \end{table}

The depression-index point estimate rises from \CoefDepTWFE~standard deviations (SE \SEDepTWFE) in the baseline to \CoefDepSibFE~SD (SE \SEDepSibFE) under the simple mother-FE design, and remains close to that magnitude with the additional community fixed effect and on the more restrictive three-or-more-sibling subsample. The larger within-mother estimate is consistent with time-invariant village confounders partly masking the program's effect on adult mental health at the cross-sectional level.

The health-index estimate flips sign: all three sibling-FE specifications return negative point estimates between $-0.16$ and $-0.26$ standard deviations, against $+\CoefHealthCS$~SD on the aggregated CS column. None of the three is individually distinguishable from zero, and the standard errors are roughly twice as large as those on the cross-sectional health regression because the within-mother contrast discards the cross-community variation that supplies most of the program's identifying signal. The negative point estimates are therefore better read as a precision collapse around zero on a much smaller subsample than as direct evidence that siblings born after the midwife's arrival fared worse on the adult health index. The within-mother comparison holds all time-invariant family characteristics constant and identifies off the difference in exposure across siblings, which is a different estimand from the cohort-average effect the main table reports. A fertility-selection response to the midwife's arrival, checked below, is one specific mechanism that could generate such a divergence; the broader interpretation, that the two estimators recover different treatment effects when effects are heterogeneous across families, is revisited in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Fertility-selection check}

Mothers did not change their fertility timing in response to the midwife's arrival. Table~\ref{tab:fertility} regresses the number of children each mother had in the 1984--1999 cohort window on whether her reproductive years (ages 15--40) overlapped with the midwife's tenure in her community of birth; the coefficient of $0.059$ children (SE $0.040$) is statistically indistinguishable from zero, and the positive direction is in any case partly age-compositional, since younger mothers mechanically have both more program overlap and more births inside the observation window. The sibling-FE comparison in Table~\ref{tab:sibling_fe} is therefore not contaminated by selective birth-timing.

\subsection{Sensitivity}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness} reports four alternative versions of the main Callaway--Sant'Anna specification, each cell the dynamic ATT averaged over event-times $0$--$3$. Column~R1 weights the CS estimator by the inverse propensity of early treatment, a standard correction for non-random placement. Column~R2 swaps the SAR-recorded rollout year for the alternative PKK measure (the two sources disagree on about 83~percent of communities for which both are recorded). Columns~R3 and~R4 swap the main non-migrant birthplace for the preceding-wave variant (which keeps internal migrants by inferring birthplace from the mother's community at the survey wave closest in time before the child's birth) and the mother-never-mover variant. Across the four columns the qualitative pattern holds: health and cognition are positive in all four, depression is small and mixed in sign, and socioemotional is negative in three of four. The mother-never-mover sample sharpens the cognition and socioemotional estimates into statistical significance, consistent with the lower migration-induced measurement error in that subsample.

\subsection{Placebo tests}

Two placebo designs examine whether the main estimates can be reproduced by moving the cohort window away from the early-life critical period. Table~\ref{tab:placebo_alt_cohorts} reports both. Column~P1 shifts the recorded rollout year five years earlier and refits CS: the placebo treated cohort is then aged roughly five to eight at the actual midwife arrival, outside the in-utero-to-three window the program is hypothesized to act through. Column~P2 redefines the exposure indicator as ``child aged 11--15 at midwife arrival'' and refits TWFE: these children are biologically too old to benefit from midwife-mediated early-life inputs.

\begin{table}[!htbp] \centering \caption{Alternative-cohort placebos.} \label{tab:placebo_alt_cohorts} \input{tables/placebo_alt_cohorts.tex} \begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth} \footnotesize \emph{Notes.} P1: Callaway--Sant'Anna dynamic ATT averaged over event-times $0$--$3$, with $G$ shifted five years earlier than the SAR-recorded rollout year. P2: TWFE coefficient on an indicator for ``child aged 11--15 at midwife arrival'', with community-of-birth, birth-year, and survey-wave fixed effects. Standard errors clustered at the community-of-birth level. Significance: $^{*}\,p<0.10$, $^{**}\,p<0.05$, $^{***}\,p<0.01$. \end{minipage} \end{table}

The older-cohort placebo passes cleanly on all four outcomes, with point estimates between $-0.06$ and $+0.05$ standard deviations and no $|t|$-statistic above $1$, supporting the interpretation that the program operates through an early-life biological channel rather than a contemporaneous village-level co-trend. The earlier-rollout placebo passes on health, depression, and socioemotional outcomes; the cognition placebo turns marginally negative ($-0.253$, SE $0.102$), consistent with cohorts born shortly before midwife arrival in eventually-treated villages having slightly lower adult cognition than cohorts in never-treated villages.

\subsection{Alternative cognition outcome}

Table~\ref{tab:child_raven} reports the cognition coefficient using the child Raven score from IFLS Book~EK, which is administered only to respondents aged 7--14 at wave~5. The estimate is essentially zero on a substantially smaller sample, consistent with the null result on the adult IRT-based cognition index in the pooled specification.
