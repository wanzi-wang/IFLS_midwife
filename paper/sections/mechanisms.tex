\section{Mechanisms} \label{sec:mechanisms}

The reduced-form estimates in Section~\ref{sec:results} tell a story about whether early-life exposure to a village midwife moves adult outcomes; they are silent about the pathway through which the program operates. Because the midwife bundle is a composite of prenatal counselling, skilled attendance at delivery, postnatal follow-up, and micronutrient distribution, the reduced-form coefficient conflates every channel into one number. The existing literature has made progress on isolating individual channels, \textcite{thomas2001} documents post-program increases in maternal body-mass index, and \textcite{frankenberg2005} tie the program's arrival to improvements in height-for-age among children born after a midwife was available in the community, but no prior study has systematically decomposed the program's reduced-form effect across the candidate channels that the IFLS data make visible. This section does exactly that, using mother-reported pregnancy-history data in IFLS book~4 module~CH to recover three mediators measured at the level of each child's own birth: (i) whether the mother had any prenatal check-up during the pregnancy; (ii) whether the delivery was attended by a trained provider (doctor, midwife, or paramedic/nurse) rather than a traditional birth attendant, a family member, or no one at all; and (iii) whether breastfeeding was ever initiated. These are the three channels that are both plausibly reachable by a village midwife and cleanly measurable across the 1984--1999 cohort in the pregnancy history recorded at the nearest-in-time IFLS wave; trimester-specific visit frequency, tetanus-toxoid injections, iron supplementation, and child immunisation registers are available only for a sharply restricted subset of the cohort and are not pursued here.

\subsection{First-stage estimates}

Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_first_stage} reports the first-stage regressions of each mediator on the program-exposure indicator $E_{ic}$, holding constant the same community-of-birth and birth-year fixed effects and the same mother controls used in the main specification. The baseline mean of prenatal check-up is \num{0.936}: essentially every mother in the sample reports at least one prenatal visit, which is the usual finding in Indonesian survey data and which leaves very little room for a marginal intervention to move the mean. Consistent with the ceiling, the first-stage estimate on prenatal check-up is a precisely-estimated zero (\num{-0.015}, SE \num{0.026}). Ever-breastfed is similarly near- universal at \num{0.980} and the point estimate is small and slightly negative (\num{-0.027}, SE \num{0.016}). The one mediator with meaningful variation in the baseline distribution is skilled birth attendance, whose sample mean is \num{0.634}: sixty-three percent of the primary-sample births were attended by a trained provider. The first-stage estimate for this mediator is \num{0.069} (SE \num{0.052}), which corresponds to about a seven-percentage-point higher probability of a trained birth among children exposed to the program in utero or before age three. The coefficient fails to reach statistical significance at conventional levels, but it is the largest in the table in both absolute magnitude and relative-to-mean terms, and its direction is consistent with the program's core service offering.

\subsection{Decomposition}

The magnitude of the first stage is only half of the channel story. The second half is how much of the program's reduced-form effect on each adult outcome is accounted for by variation in the mediator. Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_decomposition} reports, for every outcome--mediator combination, both the reduced-form (``total'') coefficient estimated on the restricted sub-sample of children whose mothers answered the pregnancy-history question and the mediator-conditional (``direct'') coefficient from a regression that adds the mediator as a covariate. The share mediated column is the standard Baron--Kenny quantity $(\hat{\beta}_{\text{total}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{direct}})/\hat{\beta}_{\text{total}}$ expressed in percent.

The single cell of the table that is quantitatively informative about the mechanism is skilled birth attendance as a mediator for the health index: the conditional coefficient falls from \num{0.021} to \num{0.019}, implying a share-mediated of about twelve percent. Put differently, of the already-small reduced-form effect on adult health in this sub-sample, roughly one-eighth is statistically accounted for by variation in whether the birth itself was attended by a trained provider. That is a small share in absolute terms, but it is the sharpest piece of evidence in the table that the canonical-channel hypothesis, the village midwife matters because she shows up at the birth, does some work on the health outcome. The other entries in the decomposition are either noise-dominated (depression and socioemotional rows show share-mediated values close to zero because the mediator-adjusted coefficient barely moves) or visibly unstable (cognition and negative-share-mediated entries for prenatal care or breastfeeding arise because the reduced-form and direct coefficients are of different signs in a sub-sample where neither is distinguishable from zero).

\subsection{Caveats}

Three limitations of this mechanism analysis warrant explicit acknowledgement. First, the pregnancy-history module was not administered to every mother in the primary sample: coverage is about sixty percent for each of the three mediators, and the analytic sub-sample drops from \NPrimary~to roughly \num{1800}--\num{2500} children depending on the mediator. The sub-sample is non-randomly selected in ways that are visible in the table, for example, the reduced-form depression effect on the restricted sample is \num{0.28}~SD against \num{0.10}~SD on the full primary sample, and the decomposition should therefore be read as suggestive of the mechanism rather than as a bias-corrected channel estimate. Second, the Baron--Kenny decomposition assumes that the mediator is exogenous conditional on the covariates in the regression, which is the classical sequential-ignorability assumption of the mediation literature; it is not a causal identification of the indirect pathway \autocite{imai2010}. Third, mother-reported retrospective data on pregnancies ten to thirteen years before the interview (for the earliest cohort, 1984--1987) carries recall bias that may attenuate the first stage for precisely the cohorts in which the program's effect is identified off the newly-treated communities. Taken together, these limitations support a cautious reading of the skilled-attendance channel as plausible but not formally identified, and leave the other two mediators as consistent with a ceiling-limited or recall-noisy null.
